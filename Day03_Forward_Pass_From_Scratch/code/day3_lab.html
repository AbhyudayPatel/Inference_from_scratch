03

top-p anatomy — the nucleus, visualized

cumulative probability mass vs rank

Candidates sorted best-first (log-rank axis); the curve is their running total after top-k. The amber line is your p — everything left of the green cutoff survives. Drag temperature and watch the same p admit 2 tokens on peaked prompts but hundreds on flat ones: that adaptivity is why nucleus sampling exists.
04

top-k anatomy — a hard truncation

mass captured by the first k tokens

No adaptivity here: k tokens are kept whether they hold 99% or 20% of the mass. Flat distribution + small k = valid alternatives get probability zero (top-p applies on top, after renorm).

09

accuracy & things to try

Where the numbers come from
sampler_server.py loads the Day-1 weights, Day-2 tokenizer and Day-3 forward pass — the same pipeline that passes the golden checksum — and ships the top-512 raw logits plus exact full-vocab grids: logZ(T), H(T), nucleus-size(p). Bar probabilities use the full-vocab Z, so they are true probabilities. Only while the repetition penalty is on does the page renormalize over the shown 512 (it says so, and the coverage stat shows the mass involved).
Things to try
“The capital of France is” — peaked: nucleus @ p=0.9 is a handful of tokens and temperature barely matters until T>1. Then “The meaning of life is” or “And then,” — flat: the nucleus explodes to hundreds, top-k=10 destroys most of the mass, entropy climbs toward the uniform ceiling. Finally set seed=42, draw 1, then shrink top-p and draw again — same seed, different pick, because the candidate set changed. That is the entire sampling story.
